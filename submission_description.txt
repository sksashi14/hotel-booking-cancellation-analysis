This report documents my Week 6 capstone: a complete data science pipeline built on the public Hotel Booking Demand dataset, which holds 119,390 bookings from a city hotel and a resort hotel in Portugal. I chose a hospitality problem because I am studying hotel management alongside computer science, and the question I asked was which bookings are likely to be cancelled and what a hotel can do about it.

The report starts with data cleaning. I handled missing values column by column, removed about 32,000 duplicate rows (which moved the cancellation rate from 37% to 27.5%), dropped impossible records such as zero-guest bookings, and capped extreme daily rates using the IQR rule. I then engineered features such as total nights, guest count and a season index, and I excluded columns that leak the answer, such as reservation status.

The exploratory analysis uses annotated Matplotlib and Seaborn charts to show seasonality, lead time effects, and cancellation differences by market segment and deposit type. For unsupervised learning I applied K-Means with elbow and silhouette checks and chose four guest segments, while stating plainly that the silhouette score of 0.22 is low. For supervised learning I compared logistic regression with a Random Forest using 5-fold cross-validation. The Random Forest reached a test ROC-AUC of 0.851 and recall of 48% on cancellations.

The report ends with business recommendations, limitations and a personal reflection. The complete reproducible Python script is included in Appendix A and in my GitHub repository.
